Evolutionary Psychology · Empirical Study

37 Cultures Study

Sex differences in human mate preferences, tested across 10,047 participants in 37 cultures on six continents, revealing what is universal and what varies.

Buss (1989) · Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 12, 1–49

This data comes from a self-reported survey, not behavioral observation. Participants were asked to rate the importance of 18 mate characteristics when choosing a long-term partner.

Surveys were administered locally in the native language across 37 cultures by ~50 collaborating researchers. Most samples were drawn from university students, averaging ~272 participants per culture (total N = 10,047). Because samples skew young and educated, they may not fully represent their broader population.

Finding
Most universal finding, confirmed in all 37 samples (p < .001). Men preferred mates an average of 2.66 years younger; women preferred mates 3.42 years older. Where marriage records were available, the actual age gap averaged 2.99 years, sitting between the two expressed preferences. Predicted direction: men prefer younger, women prefer older. The Five Hypotheses

Buss (1989) derived predictions from evolutionary theory and sexual selection. Each hypothesis was tested against the full 37-culture dataset.
H1 Good Financial Prospect
Women should value earning capacity and financial resources more than men. Resources enable offspring investment; ancestral women mating with resource-rich men gained survival advantages for their children.
Supported in 36 / 37 samples · Sole exception: Spain (virtually tied)
H2 Ambition & Industriousness
Women should value ambition more than men, as a cue to a mate's likely future resource acquisition. Ambition signals willingness to invest across time, not just present wealth.
Supported in 29 / 37 samples · Reversals: Iran, S. Africa (Zulu)
H3 Age Preference
Men should prefer younger mates (peak reproductive value); women should prefer older mates (greater resource accumulation). Everywhere men preferred women younger than themselves; women preferred men 3–4 years older on average.
Supported in all 37 samples, the most universal finding
H4 Good Looks
Men should value physical attractiveness more than women. Appearance cues fertility and health; because ancestral men could not directly assess ovulatory status, they relied on observable cues correlated with reproductive value.
Supported in 34 / 37 samples · Near-zero gap: Zulu, Poland, Sweden
H5 Chastity
Men should value chastity more than women because paternity certainty was a recurring adaptive problem in human evolutionary history. This is the most culturally variable prediction; cultural norms around sexuality substantially modulate the baseline difference.
Supported in 23 / 37 samples · Most culturally variable characteristic
