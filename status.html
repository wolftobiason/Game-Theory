Status is a signal
that travels without you

In mating markets, status does two jobs. Short-term, it decides who initiates and who responds. Long-term, it sets the size of the option pool — who gets the first look. But status only counts if it can be transmitted: compressed into a sentence someone can say about a person when they're not in the room.

Part II

The Wrap

A "wrap" is the one-sentence version of a person that circulates without them — what a friend says when asked "so what's he like?" Traits only generate status if they survive that compression. Three axes decide whether a trait travels:

Axis 1Legibility

Does it compress into one vivid sentence? "He surfs" travels — it implies a body, a lifestyle, and a place. "He's smart" doesn't — it's an abstraction with no image attached. Illegible traits can be real and still generate zero status, because no one can repeat them.

Axis 2Visibility

Is it ever witnessed? A skill practiced alone in private is socially invisible no matter how rare it is. A stage, a lineup, a court, a race — anything with an audience converts skill into status. No witnesses, no transmission.

Axis 3Stage of effect

Door-openers work at minute one — they start conversations and earn the first look. Slow burns (a serious career, depth, competence) work at month two — they convert interest into commitment. Most traits are one or the other; a strong portfolio needs both.

Part III

Why It Works

Status isn't arbitrary social fashion — it's load-bearing information in a market where direct evaluation is expensive.

Preselection

The strongest evidence that a man is desirable is that other women already treat him as desirable. Mate-choice copying is documented across species — evaluation is costly, so borrowing others' verdicts is efficient. Status is preselection made public.

Costly signaling

Status is hard to fake because it's expensive to acquire — years of skill, social capital, or achievement. That expense is the point: like the peacock's tail, the cost is what makes the signal honest, and honesty is what makes it persuasive.

Resource access

Across the 37-cultures data, women weight status and resource cues more heavily than men do — consistent with the asymmetric costs of reproduction. Status predicts future access: allies, protection, provisioning, options. It's a forecast, not a trophy.
