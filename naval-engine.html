02 · Foundation
Find Your Specific Knowledge

Specific knowledge cannot be trained for. It's found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion, not what's hot right now. Answer honestly — this is for you.

Q 01
What could you talk about for hours without getting paid, and people would still seek you out for?
Hint: this isn't "coding" or "finance" — go one level deeper. What specifically?
Q 02
What do you understand better than most people you know — and why do you think that is?
Hint: think about what others ask YOU for help on, even when you haven't promoted yourself as an expert.
Q 03
What is the intersection of skills you have that is unusual — even if no individual skill is unique?
Hint: 1-in-100 × 1-in-100 = 1-in-10,000. What's your stack?
Q 04
If you had 5 years and no financial pressure, what would you build or research?
Hint: ignore what's "practical." This reveals your actual drive.
Q 05
What is something you believe that most people in your field would disagree with?
Hint: contrarian views built on real understanding are the seeds of specific knowledge.

03 · Amplification
Audit Your Leverage Stack

Leverage is what turns your specific knowledge into wealth. Code and content are permissionless — no one can stop you from using them. Rate your current leverage honestly.

Permissionless · Start here
Code
Software works while you sleep. A function deployed once can serve millions. No one can revoke your access to this lever.
Permissionless · Start here
Media
Writing, audio, video. One piece of content can reach a million people at zero marginal cost. No one can stop you from publishing.
Permissioned · Build this second
Capital
Money working for you. Requires someone to trust you with it first. Amplifies everything — but only after the permissionless levers are in place.
Labor · Oldest leverage

"Managing people is incredibly hard. It's the oldest form of leverage — but when you use it, you're essentially operating through other people's time, not your own ideas. I would probably caution you away from it early on." — Naval Ravikant

"The most important thing to do is to be a continuous learner. If you're not learning, you're stagnating. And everyone else is learning, so you're falling behind."
