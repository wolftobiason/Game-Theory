The short game runs on attention.

Long-term mating markets don't select on who lights up a room — they select through due diligence, over long horizons, across changing circumstances. Four structures decide who wins: the vouch, the option book, whoever solves the transience discount, and the patience to let honest ties converge.

Part I

The Vouch

The Ladder measures reach — how far a signal travels. Long-term markets add a second axis: valence — what the signal says when it arrives, and whether anyone stakes their own name on it. Reach wins attention; valence survives vetting.

Reach →
Valence →

The Beloved Figure

High reach · positive valence

Known widely, spoken of warmly. Maximum long-term market power — the wrap arrives pre-endorsed, everywhere at once.

The Well-Vouched

Low reach · positive valence

Few people know him — but everyone who does speaks well of him, and some actively advocate. The marriage-market sleeper. Passes every background check.

The Notorious

High reach · negative valence

Everyone knows him; the reports are bad. Volume without trust — short-term traffic, long-term veto. Reach amplifies the damage.

The Invisible

Low reach · neutral-to-negative

No signal at all. Not vetoed — just never proposed. Every opportunity must be generated cold, in person, alone.

Why valence dominates long-term

Short-term selection happens in the room, on visible signals. Long-term selection is due diligence: she asks friends, mutuals, anyone with history. The verdict that matters is delivered when the candidate isn't present.

That's why the referral is the strongest move in the market: a friend who sets two people up is staking their own reputation — a costly signal no self-presentation can imitate — on a match they've already screened from both sides.

Referral requirement 1Describability

“You should meet him — he ___.” The sentence has to complete itself. The wrap still matters in the long game; advocates need material to advocate with.

Referral requirement 2Confidence

The advocate is spending their own credibility. They only spend it on someone they're certain will treat the other person well and reflect well on them. Years of small reliabilities fund this account.

Referral requirement 3Known availability

The silent failure mode: well-liked, fully vouched, and receiving zero referrals — because nobody knows he's looking. The advocacy channel has to be switched on.

The Negativity Veto

Vetting is loss-dominated: one credible bad report — flaky, cruel in a breakup, unreliable — can cancel years of good ones. “Nobody has anything bad to say about him” isn't the absence of a compliment; in this market, it is the credential. The clean record beats the more impressive record with one stain.

Part II

The Option Book

Long horizons change the math of every connection. A genuine tie kept honestly warm — across cities, countries, circumstances — is a long-dated option: nearly free to maintain, impossible to buy later, exercisable only if both lives converge. Nobody can predict which ties converge. That's precisely why a book of them beats any single bet.

Rule 1Cheap to hold

An occasional genuine message, a real conversation when paths cross. If maintaining a tie feels like a campaign, it's mispriced — the whole point of an option is that the carrying cost is near zero.

Rule 2States change

People leave relationships, change cities, change countries, change minds. A book built this year is exercised years from now, under circumstances nobody currently holds. The option's value lives in that uncertainty.

Rule 3Honest premium only

The tie must be real — a friendship that would be worth having even if nothing ever converged. A tie held under false pretenses is discovered, and the discovery converts an option into a negative vouch that travels through her whole network.

The distant tie

Live · long-dated

Met abroad, real rapport, different countries. Worth nothing this month; potentially everything in five years. Converges only if someone moves — which people do.

The committed tie

Frozen · not exercisable

Currently in a relationship. The honest move is genuine, agenda-free friendship or distance — nothing else. Most relationships in a lifetime end; the friendship that behaved well is remembered when one does.

The dormant tie

Dormant · re-openable

Warm history, long silence. Re-opens at the cost of one honest message — the cheapest re-entry in the book. Dormant ties famously outperform: history plus fresh information.

The network tie

Live · compounding

Not a prospect — a friend who knows prospects. One well-vouched connection into a new circle generates referrals for years. Often the highest-value position in the book.

The local anchor

Live · place-bound

A tie to a place — a community, a scene, a city returned to yearly. Anchors make every other tie in that geography cheaper to hold and more credible to re-open.

The expired tie

Closed

Interest confirmed absent, or the tie went cold beyond honest revival. Closing cleanly is a vouch in itself — the network remembers who handled a no gracefully.

Part III

The Transience Discount

The standard objection to the traveler: “why invest in someone who's leaving?” It's not an excuse — it's rational pricing. Long-term interest is an investment, and an investment needs a horizon. A short local clock collapses expected value no matter how good the in-person signal is: charisma is experienced only in person, so it stops compounding the moment the plane leaves. What survives distance is structure — status that travels, and credible reasons the clock isn't really short.

Local horizon
How long until he leaves — as far as she can tell.
Status tier (see: The Ladder)
How far his signal travels without him. High tiers are worth knowing from anywhere — the national-champion effect: geography stops being an objection because the connection itself is the prize.
Return credibility
Structural reasons the departure isn't final — recurring work, family ties, a community he demonstrably comes back to.

Offset 1Status that travels

Scene status usually stops at the scene's border — but national-tier status inverts the problem entirely: the connection is valuable regardless of geography, so “he's leaving” stops mattering. Below that tier, status softens the discount; it doesn't erase it.

Offset 2Credible return structure

“I might be back” is noise. “I'm here every summer — my closest friends are here” is structure: verifiable, costly, and it converts a departure into a recurrence. The discount prices the horizon she can verify, not the one he claims.

Offset 3Reframe the channel

If the local clock can't be extended, stop selling a local relationship. The honest alternative is the option book: a real correspondence with no demanded outcome. It asks for no investment at short-horizon prices — which is exactly why it's accepted.

Part IV

Convergence

A tie is never exercised the day it's made. It's exercised years later, on the rare occasion that everything lines up at once: both people single, both at a life stage that wants the same thing, geography willing to move, and the timing simply right. Nobody can predict which tie converges — which is the entire argument for holding a book of them rather than betting on one. But first, a tie only counts if it passed the read.

The Read — the entry gate

Not every contact is an option. A contact becomes a real position in the book only if it survives a fast character screen — the goal isn't to rate people, it's to not fool yourself. Thin-slice judgment is reliable for warmth and intelligence, and for one dark trait in particular — but it's asymmetric, and knowing the asymmetry is the whole skill.

Stage 1 · The Alarm

Trust the hit

Grandiose narcissism leaks at first contact — dominance, status-jockeying, the conversation always bending back. When the detector fires, it's a true positive. Act on it; don't argue yourself out of a real red flag. Presence is strong evidence.

Stage 2 · The Silence

Don't trust the quiet

A clean read isn't a clean person. Covert narcissism presents as shy and sensitive; the grandiose kind often wears charm. The miss, not the false alarm, is the danger. Absence is weak evidence — “nothing yet,” not “safe.”

Stage 3 · The Clock

Let time break the tie

Repeated contact is the instrument the thin slice lacks: narcissists get less likable over exposure; kind people hold or improve. A clean first read only clears once consistency over time confirms it. Then it enters the book.

The Screening Rule

Trust the alarm, distrust the silence, and let time break the tie. The cost structure justifies a hair-trigger: you have many options, so the price of a false alarm (skipping one good person) is trivial, while the price of a miss (years lost to a narcissist) is catastrophic. A hair-trigger on this one flag is rational, not paranoid.

The Convergence Engine
